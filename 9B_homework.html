⏳ Present simple vs present continuous

Theory and examples only. You will practise on the next screens.

Present simple

We use it for habits, routines and true facts — things that happen again and again, or always.

every dayusuallyalwaysneveron Mondays

Present continuous

We use it for actions happening now, at this moment, or around now (this week, today).

nowright nowat the momenttodayLook!

The same person, two tenses:
Anna wears a police uniform every day. → her normal routine.
Today Anna is wearing jeans and a green jumper. → different, only today.

Present simple

I / you / we / they + verb  •  he / she / it + verbs
I / you / we / theywear a uniform.
he / she / itwears a uniform.

Spelling of -s: work → workswear → wearswatch → watchesgo → goesstudy → studies

Present continuous

am / is / are + verb-ing
Iam wearing a coat.
he / she / itis wearing a coat.
you / we / theyare wearing coats.

Spelling of -ing: work → workingwear → wearingwrite → writingrun → runningsit → sitting

Short forms: I’mhe’sshe’swe’rethey’re

Word order in questions: the helper verb comes first.
Does she wear…? · Is she wearing…?
After does / doesn’t the verb has no -s.

1. Some verbs are not usually continuous.
likelovehatewantneedknowunderstandbelieveremember
✓ She likes her job. ✗ She is liking her job.
2. Don’t forget the helper verb.
✗ She wearing a red dress. → ✓ She is wearing a red dress.
3. No -s after does / doesn’t.
✗ He doesn’t wears gloves. → ✓ He doesn’t wear gloves.
4. wear vs put on
put on = the action (a moment). wear = the state (clothes on your body).
5. Singular and plural clothes words.
a hat, a coat, a dress, a shirt → is
jeans, trousers, shoes, boots, gloves, sunglasses → always plural: are, no a

📖 The rule

Present simple

for habits, routines and facts

he / she / it + verbs

Anna wears a uniform every day.
She doesn’t wear jeans at work.
Does she work on Sundays?

Present continuous

for actions now and around now

am / is / are + verbing

Today she is wearing jeans.
She isn’t following anyone now.
Is she working undercover?

Remember: some verbs are not usually continuous — {{like}}, love, {{know}}, {{want}}, {{need}}, {{understand}}. ✓ She likes her job. ✗ She is liking her job.
